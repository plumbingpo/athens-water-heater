Keeping Your Winder Water Heater Running Longer

Most early failures trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or moisture around the base, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
