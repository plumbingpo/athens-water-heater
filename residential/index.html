Upfront Pricing

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Before we pick up a wrench, we figure out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians check your heater's age and gallon capacity, the burner or elements, the relief valve, the venting, and any rust or seepage around the fittings. Then you get a straightforward recommendation and the complete price up front, whether the job is replacing a thermocouple or replacing the whole unit. No hidden fees, no last-minute add-ons.

The problem with water heater failures is that most of them develop inside the tank where you can't see them. Hard-water minerals build a crust on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod gets eaten away gradually, and when it's gone, the tank itself starts to corrode. When hot water comes out rusty, the tank rumbles, or you spot moisture under the heater, have it checked before a repair turns into a replacement.

Not every visit is about a breakdown. Plenty of homeowners ask us to size up an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with annual descaling), a replacement on your own timeline beats a rushed swap after a leak. We'll give you an honest read on how much life your equipment has left.

Preventive Care

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

The best repair is the one you never need. That's the whole point of our maintenance plans. On each scheduled visit, we flush the sediment out of the tank, check the anode rod, exercise the relief valve, inspect the venting and gas connections or the electrical components, and run a descaling cycle on tankless units, all matched to the heater in your home.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and more frequent flushing where the water is hard. That's especially important in hard-water areas, where sediment insulates the tank bottom, makes the burner work harder, and shortens the life of the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and our technicians tailor the plan to your equipment's age, your household's hot water needs, and your local water.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a consistent maintenance schedule pays off. A clean tank heats water faster and more quietly, a healthy anode rod keeps rust from attacking the steel, and a working relief valve protects the whole house. Most families find that annual service costs far less than replacing a water heater even a few years early.

Proper Sizing

The Right Size Water Heater for Your Home

An undersized water heater runs out before everyone has showered, while an oversized one burns fuel keeping extra gallons hot around the clock. Athens Water Heater sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual household. For tank models, that means matching the first-hour rating to your busiest hour, rather than simply copying the gallon size on the old tank. For tankless models, it means sizing the gallons per minute against the fixtures you run at once and how cold your incoming water gets in winter.

Households change over time. A new baby, a teenager, or an in-law suite can outgrow the tank that used to be plenty. Tankless water heaters heat water on demand and don't run out as long as the flow stays within its GPM rating, while hybrid heat pump tanks use far less electricity than a standard electric tank. If you're torn between tank and tankless, or gas and electric, we'll explain the honest pros and cons in installed price, operating cost, venting and gas line needs, and installation work.

Common Problems

Hot Water Problems We Solve Every Week

Homes in Athens run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Athens Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that only gets lukewarm, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks with burned-out elements, noisy tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

The correct fix depends entirely on the part that failed. On electric tanks, it's usually an element or thermostat. On gas units, it tends to be a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure spikes get solved with an expansion tank and a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and leaks at the drain valve or fittings get repaired before the damage spreads. We also verify the thermostat setting, since water above 120°F can scald young children and older adults in seconds. If the tank itself is leaking, no repair will hold, and we'll say so plainly. And if you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility right away.
