How Jefferson Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Many early failures trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular service, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like discolored hot water, rumbling, or a damp spot around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water begins corroding the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a flooded utility room.
