Going tankless changes how your home makes hot water. There is no tank to keep hot, so no energy is spent reheating stored water while you sleep or travel. When a faucet or shower opens, the unit senses flow and fires, heating water as it moves through a heat exchanger. The result is a continuous supply for as long as you stay within the unit's rating, a compact footprint on the wall, and a lifespan that can be nearly double that of a typical tank. Homeowners in Athens, GA often choose tankless during a replacement or remodel for exactly those reasons.

The most important step happens before anything is installed. We total your peak demand in gallons per minute, such as a shower, a kitchen sink, and the dishwasher at once, and compare it against how much the unit must heat cold winter water. That tells us the real capacity you need. Next we check the supply side. Gas tankless units fire at very high input, so a half-inch line that fed an old tank is often too small. Condensing models vent through PVC or polypropylene and need a condensate drain, while non-condensing units need stainless steel venting. Electric models are their own case, often needing multiple large breakers and sometimes a service upgrade.

Every tankless install from Athens Water Heater includes isolation service valves so future descaling takes minutes instead of hours, a clean gas connection with a sediment trap, correctly pitched and terminated venting, and a pressure relief valve piped to a safe location. If your water is hard, we talk through scale protection, since mineral buildup is the main enemy of a tankless heat exchanger. We can add recirculation for faster hot water at distant bathrooms, set the temperature, handle permits where required, and walk you through maintenance before we leave. Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Wanting a unit that can last up to twice as long as a typical tank

  • Standby losses on a tank that sits idle for long stretches

  • A growing household or new bathroom adding hot water demand

Why Hire a Professional for Tankless Water Heater Installation?

Why not install a tankless unit yourself? Because the risks and the details stack up quickly. A burner that can draw nearly 199,000 BTU needs properly sized gas piping, and leaks or undersized lines are both dangerous and costly. Venting has to match the unit exactly, with the right material, slope, and termination clearances, or combustion gases can end up where they do not belong. Electric units can overwhelm an older electrical panel, and warranty terms often depend on a qualified installation. Permits and inspections are common requirements as well.

Done professionally, a tankless system performs well and stays safe. We start with a real demand assessment, confirm the gas and electrical supply, and design venting that meets the manufacturer's requirements. The install includes service valves, a sediment trap, a correctly routed relief valve line, and a neutralized condensate drain where needed, then a test with several fixtures running. Just as important, we tell you plainly if a tank, hybrid, or smaller point-of-use unit would serve you better. The goal is the right water heater, not just a tankless sale.

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Keep the air intake and vent termination clear of snow, leaves, and debris
  • Protect outdoor or garage units from freezing, and keep power on during cold snaps so freeze protection works
  • If the unit shows a recurring code, note it and have it checked
  • Flush the unit with a descaling solution annually to keep scale off the heat exchanger
  • Check and rinse the small filter screen at the cold inlet periodically
  • Avoid cranking the temperature higher than you need; about 120F works for most homes
  • Consider a water softener or scale-prevention filter if your water is hard
